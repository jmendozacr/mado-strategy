## What is CRT

Candle 1 is your range. Mark its high and low.

Candle 2 sweeps one side and closes its BODY back inside the range.

Candle 3 delivers to the other side.

C2 takes the high -> target the low. Takes the low -> target the high.

**The body close is everything.** A wick back inside the range is not enough. Candle 2 has to
close its body inside candle 1's range. No body close, no CRT.

[Chart: a 3-candle schematic, C1/C2/C3, with CRTH/CRTL marked at C1's high/low; a second schematic
showing a 2-candle case (C1/C2 only) with crth/crtl.]

It doesn't have to be 3 candles. It can be 5, 10, even 100 candles. As long as the swept high (or
low) stays protected, the CRT is still valid and still delivers.

Don't trade a candle that hasn't taken anything. If a candle takes no high and no low, it's not
your C2. You wait. The setup only exists after a sweep.

[Chart: EURUSD daily, a multi-candle CRT example.]

## CRT is fractal

It exists on every timeframe: yearly, 6-month, monthly, weekly, daily, all the way down. BTC on the
12-month chart prints the same three-candle pattern you see on the 15 minute. And on every asset.
Forex, crypto, indices, gold. US30 daily: CRT inside CRT inside CRT. Same logic everywhere.

[Chart: BTC/USDT 12-month candles showing a 3-bar CRT shape.]

## How I actually use it: top-down

HTF CRT = your bias. LTF CRT = your entry. No clear daily bias? No lower timeframe trades. The
daily is the filter for everything.

## Key levels — only 3

1. Fair value gap
2. Old high / old low
3. Rejection block

Nothing else.

### Key level 1: FVG

When price taps an FVG, don't short the very next candle. Wait for a candle to take the previous
candle's high and close back inside. That's your C2. As long as that high holds, the CRT is valid.

[Chart: BTC/USDT daily with an FVG zone marked.]

### Key level 2: old high / old low

BTC takes an old high. The next candle closes back inside the range. That's a CRT short, targeting
the low.

[Chart: BTC/USDT daily, old-high sweep example.]

Same thing, flipped. Old low gets taken. Mark the high and low of the candle that took it, then
wait. The next candle sweeps its low and closes back inside -> longs, as long as that low holds.

[Chart: BTC/USDT daily, old-low sweep example with an FVG.]

## With trend vs. against trend

Top priority is always with-trend CRTs. I only take against-trend CRTs when price has an FVG to
fill. That's the only exception.

CRT inside a CRT = A++. Two CRTs stacked at the same level, with an FVG to fill. Those are the
setups I'm most confident in, even against the trend.

[Chart: BTC/USDT daily, an old-low sweep with a nested CRT.]

Every entry is an exit, and every exit is an entry. Where one CRT completes, the next one often
starts. One move ends, the next setup forms right there.

[Chart: BTC/USDT daily, consecutive CRTs at an old low.]

Book profits at CRT highs and lows. Weekly -> 4H, daily -> 1H, 4H -> 15m. 1:3 is more than enough.
You don't need to hold for HTF liquidity every time.

[Chart: BTC/USDT daily, FVG + old-low example with profit-booking levels marked.]

## The only time a with-trend CRT doesn't deliver: SMT

GBPUSD weekly: closed back inside, SMT formed, never reached the low, and flipped straight into a

## Key level 3: rejection block

A rejection block is a failed change in state of delivery. Price taps a series of candles, fails to
close through them, and those candle bodies become support or resistance.

[Chart: EURUSD weekly, a rejection-block zone marked after an old-high sweep.]

Candle bodies matter more than wicks. BTC daily: price never closed a body through the rejection
block. Everyone shorting was targeting the liquidity below. It never came. Price bounced off the
body.

[Chart: EURUSD daily, a rejection block holding as support.]

So book your first TP at candle bodies, not at liquidity. Watch the reaction there. If a new setup
forms, then target the liquidity.

[Chart: BTC/USDT daily, old-high + FVG + rejection block, with a TP annotation at the rejection
block's own body.]
